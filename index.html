1What you're trying to do

Simple Wars is a real-time strategy game. Every side starts with a few towns, a small army and some money. You grow your economy, raise more troops and take ground until the enemy is beaten.

In a skirmish you choose how the match is won under Victory on the setup screen:

  • Domination (the default): capture every town the enemy holds.
  • Annihilation: destroy every enemy unit. Unarmed trade convoys don't count.

When you meet the condition, a 15-second countdown starts. If the condition still holds when it ends, you win. If you hold no towns at all, a 15-second defeat countdown starts, so retake one quickly. In Annihilation you also lose if your whole army is destroyed while the enemy still has units.

Play vs AI offers three ways to play: a Campaign, a war fought region by region; Missions, set battles with their own briefing and objective; and a Skirmish on a new map. Campaign assaults are won like Domination. In a campaign defense battle you win by holding at least one town for 10 minutes.

2Your first few minutes

  1. Start a skirmish. Choose Play vs AI, then Skirmish. The default settings (one AI opponent, Domination, $10,000 starting money) make a good first game. Press Start Game.
  2. Read the top bar. Economy is your money and how much it changes each second. Manpower is the pool you recruit from; you begin with 5,000.
  3. Build a factory. Open Buildings, choose Factory, then click one of your towns. It costs $4,000 and takes 60 seconds. It raises that town's money income and lets it train armor and motorized infantry (it adds no manpower: a farm does).
  4. Train infantry. Open Units, choose Infantry, then click a town. The new unit appears beside the town 15 seconds later. Each town works on one order at a time, so spread your orders across your towns.
  5. Push your border. Drag a box around a few units and right-click a spot inside enemy land. Units capture ground as they move, and every land tile you own adds a little money.
  6. Stay connected. Troops need a path of your own land back to one of your towns. A yellow countdown over a unit means it is cut off. Reconnect it before the countdown runs out.
  7. Take their towns. Find the enemy's towns, clear out the defenders and occupy every built-up tile. When the last one falls, you win.

Press Space to pause whenever you need to think. You can still look around and give orders while paused.

4Money, manpower and building

You have two resources. Money ($) pays for units, buildings and upkeep. Manpower (MP) is needed to recruit units and to refill damaged ones while they heal. The top bar shows both, with their change per second.

Land income adds up: 500 tiles bring in $2 a second, so taking ground pays even before you take another town.

Trade convoys start travelling between your towns once you have two or more. Each one pays $300 when it arrives. Sea convoys need a harbor at both ends. Convoys are unarmed: an armed enemy unit that reaches one captures it, cargo and all.

Upkeep. Every unit costs a little money each second (see the unit table). If your money runs out while your upkeep is higher than your income, your units lose 2.5% of their maximum HP every second until you can pay again.

Recruiting

  • Open Units, choose a unit, then click one of your towns that can train it. Press Esc to cancel.
  • You pay the full cost at once. The unit appears beside the town when its training time is up.
  • Each town handles one order at a time: one unit or one building.
  • Press the small i on any unit or building button to see its details before you buy.

6Terrain, combat, morale and generals

Fighting

  • Units fire on their own at the nearest enemy they can see within range.
  • To choose a target, select units and click the enemy. They move into range and chase it while they can see it. If it slips into the fog, they search where it was last seen. A new move order, a retreat or Clear target on the unit card ends the chase.
  • Units in a fight slow to a third of their speed. Retreating units, and units closing on a target you chose, keep 75%.

Terrain

Ground matters. For each unit and terrain, the table shows the damage the unit deals (Atk) and the damage it takes (Dmg taken), compared with open grass. Damage taken below 100% means cover. A dash means the unit can't go there.

In a fight, the attacker's values for its own tile and its target's tile are combined, then the target's cover applies. So armor on open ground still struggles against troops dug into the hills. Select a unit while it fights to see the result under Enemy terrain effects.

Morale

  • Every unit has morale, up to 100. It drops when the unit takes damage, and slowly while it stays in a fight.
  • Low morale weakens a unit: it deals less damage and moves more slowly.
  • At 20 morale or less the unit routs. It flashes white, falls back on its own and won't take orders, though it still fires back. It rallies once its morale is back to 50 and it has been safe from pursuers and fire for 3 seconds.
  • Out of combat, morale recovers 3 points a second, or 11 a second at a town or hospital where the unit can heal.
  • Stop on the unit card (or H) halts your units where they stand.
  • Retreat on the unit card pulls your units back to a safer spot nearby.

Experience

Units earn experience (XP) by dealing damage. Gold bars above a unit show its rank. Select a unit to see its XP on the unit card.

Generals

Every side starts each battle with one general: an armor unit marked with a gold star and a purple ring. It fights like ordinary armor. Your units inside the ring get +5% attack, rate of fire, toughness, weapon range and morale, and move 10% faster. A general still commands while aboard a transport. If your general is lost, you don't get another.

7Towns, capturing and territory

Territory

  • The land is divided between the sides. Water never belongs to anyone.
  • A land unit always holds the tile it stands on, unless an enemy unit is on the same tile.
  • When a unit moves into enemy land, it also takes the tiles around it: one ring of tiles for most units, two rings for armor. Standing still at the border takes nothing, so you have to move in.
  • A tile next to an enemy unit can't be taken from a distance. Step onto it or drive the enemy away.
  • Ships never capture land.
  • Enemy land that is completely surrounded by yours, with no units, no town and no coastline inside, switches to you on its own.

Capturing a town

  • Each town covers a cluster of built-up tiles.
  • You capture it when you own every one of those tiles and none of the defender's units stand on any of them. A single tile left in enemy hands keeps the town theirs.
  • The town's tiles and the land around it then become yours. Its factory, farm and harbor each have about an even chance of being destroyed in the capture.
  • A captured town pays you at once and can train units as soon as it is no longer under attack.
  • Towns are worth fighting for: each brings income and manpower, a place to recruit and heal, and a source of supply.

8Supply, healing, hospitals and forts

Supply

  • A land unit is in supply when an unbroken path of your own land leads from its tile to one of your towns. The path can run diagonally and over finished bridges, but never over water.
  • A unit at the front, on ground it hasn't captured yet, stays in supply while your supplied land is within two tiles of it, unless enemy troops sit in the gap.
  • A friendly supply ship keeps land units near it in supply.
  • Marines are also in supply wherever their patch of land touches the sea or a river.
  • A pocket that contains one of your towns is always in supply, even when surrounded.

The attrition timer

  1. When a unit loses supply, the game waits 2 seconds to be sure.
  2. A yellow countdown from 10 then appears above the unit.
  3. When the countdown ends, the unit loses 3.5% of its maximum HP every second, or half that near one of your forts.

A unit can't heal while it is out of supply, countdown included. Getting back into supply, or boarding a transport, resets the timer. The timer runs on game time: it stops while paused and runs faster at 2x and 3x. Ordinary land units standing in water are out of supply too; ships, marines and passengers on transports are not. The same rules apply to every side, AI included.

Healing

  • Units heal only on your own town tiles or inside one of your hospitals' pink rings.
  • They recover 3% of their maximum HP a second, drawing manpower from your pool to refill their ranks. With no manpower left, they can't heal.
  • They must be in supply and standing on your own land.
  • Healing in a town stops while the town is under attack or enemy units are close. Inside a hospital ring, only units that are firing or being fired at stop healing.
  • Ships repair within two tiles of one of your town tiles, or inside a hospital ring. Other units can't heal in water.
  • A green + above a unit means it is healing.

Hospitals

A hospital costs $3,000 and takes 60 seconds to build on your own open land outside towns. It heals, but gives no supply and no defense. If the enemy captures its tile, it is destroyed, so keep hospitals behind the front.

Forts

A fort costs $3,000 and takes 60 seconds to build on your own open land outside towns. Inside its blue ring your units take 20% less damage and enemy units move 20% slower. Overlapping forts don't add up. A fort also halves supply attrition for your cut-off units close to it. Forts don't supply or heal. If the enemy captures a fort's tile, the fort is destroyed.

9The navy

Harbors

A town can build a harbor if its built-up tiles touch water, or touch a beach next to water. The harbor adds $2 a second and lets the town build warships, transports and supply ships, which launch into the water beside it.

Ships

  • Warships stay on the water and have the longest weapon range in the game. They can hit land units near the shore but never capture land.
  • Transports carry one land unit. To board, select the land unit and right-click your empty transport (or press Board); the two meet at the shore. To land, select the loaded transport and right-click the coast (or press Unload). The passenger lands on a clear tile nearby and carries on to where you clicked. Passengers can't fight or heal, but they don't suffer attrition. If the transport sinks, its passenger is lost.
  • Supply ships keep your land units inside their ring in supply, including troops that have just landed. The ring reaches about six cells, twice a fort's, so troops landed beside a coastal town can fight in its streets from the ship's cover. They don't help units swimming in the water. Like transports, they have only 50 HP, so escort them.
  • Ships repair near your towns: see Healing.

Marines

Marines train at any coastal town and don't need a harbor. They move through water at 90% speed with no attrition, fight at full strength on water and beaches, and are weak inland. They can't heal while in the water.

Other troops in water

Infantry, armor and the other land units can wade into water if you order them to, at 90% speed. They are out of supply there and take much more damage, so use transports or bridges instead.

Bridges

  • Choose Bridge under Buildings, then press on one bank and drag to the other. Tapping one bank and then the other also works. The preview shows the length, the price and anything that is wrong.
  • A bridge costs $1,000 for each water cell it crosses, up to 4 cells (fewer on a diagonal), and takes 60 seconds to build.
  • At least one bank must be your territory. The far bank can belong to the enemy.
  • A finished bridge works like open ground for movement and carries supply across, but a bridge never supplies anything on its own.
  • If neither bank is yours any more before the bridge is finished, it is cancelled and you get your money back.

11The AI opponents

You can face up to three AI opponents in a skirmish. Each one is led by a commander with a style of its own: balanced, deliberate attacker, defensive, maneuver, amphibious, highland or blitz. The style is picked at random for each match and kept secret.

AI difficulty is set in skirmish setup (missions use the same setting), when you begin a campaign (you can change it later in the campaign panel) and in a multiplayer lobby for the AI seats. Easy recruits one unit at a time, reacts slowly, attacks only with a big advantage and never uses flanking wings, supply cuts or ambushes. Normal is the full opponent. Hard reacts faster, accepts thinner odds and remembers what it saw under fog for longer.

No difficulty gives the AI extra money, troops or vision. AI commanders play by your rules, with the same costs, build times, supply, upkeep, healing and fog of war, and every AI treats you like any other enemy. Expect them to scout, defend their towns, gather troops before they attack, finish what they start and pull back when a fight goes badly.

12The battle report

Press Stats in the top bar, or the Stats tab on a phone, during a match. The report has four tabs:

  • Players: every side's land share and standing, and the AI difficulty.
  • Economy: your treasury, income, upkeep and production orders.
  • Forces: your personnel, the make-up of your army and its supply status.
  • Combat: losses, enemy casualties and territory changes.

With fog of war on, you see each enemy's land total but only the forces you have spotted; their money and production stay hidden until the match ends. When the match ends, the results show the full report for every side and the gold you earned.

13Multiplayer and your profile

Open Multiplayer from the main menu. It has four tabs:

  • Browse games: open lobbies first, then matches in progress. If you were disconnected, rejoin from here.
  • Host: name a lobby, add a password if you want a private game, and choose the number of human seats and AI bots. A match holds up to four players. In the lobby, set the map and rules, then press Launch match.
  • Friends: send a friend request using someone's player ID. Once they accept, you can invite each other to lobbies.
  • Rankings: compare wins, kills and losses, worldwide or among friends.

Multiplayer always runs at normal speed and can't be paused. If your connection drops, the AI commands your forces until you return. Surrendering from the pause menu counts as a defeat: your units leave the map, and your towns and land become neutral.

Your profile. Your progress, gold and unlocks are saved in this browser. Choose Sign in with Steam under Multiplayer → Your profile to restore them on other devices. Your display name must be unique, and your player ID is what friends use to add you. Lifetime statistics are kept separately for single player and multiplayer.

14Gold, match rewards and the shop

Gold is the shop currency. It is separate from the money you spend in battle. New profiles start with 100 gold.

Match rewards

To earn gold from a match, play for at least 60 seconds and damage an enemy at least once. Then you earn gold whether you win or lose:

ForGold
Winning10
Losing or surrendering5
Each minute played1, up to 15
Each enemy unit destroyed2, up to 20
Every 2 units you build1, up to 8

Only real playing time counts: time spent paused doesn't count, and playing at 2x or 3x doesn't earn time gold faster. If you leave a match before it ends, you get nothing for the result but keep the rest. The results screen shows the breakdown. Each achievement also pays 10 gold, once.

The shop

Spend gold in the Shop on the main menu. Everything you buy is yours to keep.

  • Troop skins: new looks for your armies and for the AI's, chosen separately. The Napoleonic, Medieval and Ancient skins also unlock that era for your matches.
  • Missions: historical missions and mission packs, played under Missions → Store.
  • Map skins: battlefield packs that restyle the whole map.
  • Frontline & command: styles for borders and order arrows.
  • Buy gold: top up your gold through Steam Wallet, or get the Supporter Pack: the exclusive Patron Gold skins for every unit in every era, plus 5,000 gold.

Every item shows its price. Standard troop skins and frontline and command styles cost 500 gold each.
