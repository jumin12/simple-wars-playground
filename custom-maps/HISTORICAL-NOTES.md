# Historical scenario notes

These are historically inspired RTS missions. Terrain, distances, army sizes, chronology and unit roles are adapted to Simple Wars. Tactical outcomes are player-driven. The game has no aircraft or minefield simulation: Midway abstracts air groups with its existing unit archetypes, and El Alamein represents defensive belts with forts. Normandy landing infantry uses marine rules to model landing craft; ordinary armor begins on supplied dry land.

- **Thermopylae: The Hot Gates — 480 BCE.** Hold the pass for four simulated minutes and guard the flanking trail. The Greek historical defeat is stated explicitly; tactical success represents a delaying action. [Herodotus on Thermopylae, translated extracts](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/herodotus/herodotus-on-thermopylae/).
- **Hastings: Senlac Ridge — 14 October 1066.** Norman attacks against the English high-ground position inform the ridge and cavalry approaches. [English Heritage battlefield history](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/1066-battle-of-hastings-abbey-and-battlefield/history-and-stories/what-happened-battle-hastings/).
- **Austerlitz: The Pratzen Heights — 2 December 1805.** The Goldbach crossings, central heights and allied southern movement inform the three-lane design. [French Ministry of the Armed Forces](https://www.defense.gouv.fr/terre/mieux-nous-connaitre/histoire-larmee-terre/larmee-terre-travers-ses-batailles/2-decembre-1805-bataille), [Fondation Napoléon tactical account](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/the-battle-of-austerlitz-and-the-principles-of-war/).
- **El Alamein: Breakthrough — 23 October–4 November 1942.** The Mediterranean coast, Qattara Depression and defensive belts inform the map. Rear objectives are labeled as sectors rather than falsely dating the subsequent capture of El Daba to the opening attack. [National Army Museum account](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-alamein).

- **Normandy: Link the Beachheads — 6–12 June 1944.** Retains the purchased `normandy-dday` ID. Required objectives are Sainte-Mère-Église, Carentan, Isigny, Bayeux and Ouistreham. Caen, Cherbourg and later regional objectives are context, not required D-Day captures. [National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/d-day), [Carentan local history](https://carentanlesmarais.fr/decouvrir/dday/).
- **Kursk: Operation Citadel — 5–12 July 1943.** Hold Kursk and Oboyan for five simulated minutes. Removing the old kill-triggered counteroffensive announcements avoids treating later operations as opening-day events. [The Tank Museum](https://tankmuseum.org/article/background-battle-kursk).
- **Midway — 4–7 June 1942.** Defeat the enemy naval force. Ship counters abstract task groups and their aircraft; the game does not claim the carrier battle was a direct surface duel. There is no artificial enemy town conquest requirement. [US Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/world-war-ii/1942/midway.html).

## Ordered campaign packs

Purchase a pack once, then play it under **Missions → Store**. Only stage one starts unlocked. A victory unlocks the next stage and saves progress locally and to the connected profile. Defeats and surrenders leave the current stage available. Unlocked stages can be replayed without consuming the pack. The end-of-battle button opens the next stage’s briefing and preview. Original single-mission IDs, prices and one-shot entitlements remain intact.

These packs connect tactical perspectives across an operation; they do not claim that the same formation fought every sector. Simultaneous battles can overlap in date. Mission durations compress historical time. Generic reinforcement announcements describe game events, not purported historical dispatches.

### Berlin 1945: The Final Offensive — 350 gold

Seven sectors: Seelow Heights (16–19 April), northeastern districts (21–23 April), Köpenick–Neukölln (23–26 April), southern districts (26–28 April), Moabit/Spree (28–29 April), Reichstag (30 April–1 May), central sectors and garrison surrender (1–2 May). Districts and individual buildings are scaled town objectives; the Reichstag’s capture is distinguished from the end of the battle and the later general German surrender.

References: [German Historical Museum](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/der-zweite-weltkrieg/kriegsverlauf/schlacht-um-berlin-1945), [Bundesarchiv situation map](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/dokumente-zur-zeitgeschichte/lagekarte-zur-schlacht-um-berlin/), [Berlin historical context](https://www.berlin.de/landesdenkmalamt/denkmale/highlight-denkmale-der-alliierten/die-alliierten-in-berlin/berlin-unter-verwaltung-der-vier-maechte-1945-1948-646239.php).

### Normandy: D-Day to Cherbourg — 300 gold

Five perspectives: Operation Tonga (6 June before dawn), Omaha (6 June), Gold/Juno/Sword (6–7 June), Carentan (10–13 June), Cherbourg (22–26 June). Airborne troops begin on land. Omaha assault infantry uses marine rules to represent landing craft, not Marine Corps troops. An explicitly labeled off-map embarkation depot is a gameplay supply anchor. Caen is not a D-Day capture. Capturing Cherbourg’s harbor objective does not make the damaged historical port immediately operational.

References: [National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/d-day), [British Army on the airborne operation](https://www.army.mod.uk/news/by-air-to-battle-on-d-day/), [ABMC on the Utah sector](https://www.abmc.gov/history/discover-the-history-of-utah-beach-american-memorial/), [Carentan](https://carentanlesmarais.fr/decouvrir/dday/), [US Army, Utah Beach to Cherbourg](https://history.army.mil/Publications/Publications-Catalog/Utah-Beach-to-Cherbourg/), [Cherbourg history](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/assault-on-fortress-cherbourg-june-1944).

### Market Garden: Bridges and Withdrawal — 300 gold

Five missions: the ground corridor (17 September), Son’s broken bridge (18–19 September), the Arnhem north-end perimeter (19–21 September), Nijmegen/Waal (20 September), and the Oosterbeek withdrawal, Operation Berlin (25–26 September). Arnhem is a timed delaying action. The final mission requires ferrying at least three original 1st Airborne formations across the Lower Rhine and unloading them after the three-minute nightfall marker; newly trained troops do not count. Winning this evacuation is not presented as a successful Allied capture of Arnhem. The Waal assault-boat units represent US Army airborne troops using marine movement rules.

References: [National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/market-garden), [UK Ministry of Defence campaign booklet](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/30056/ww2_market_garden.pdf), [Netherlands Institute of Military History](https://www.nimh.nl/militaire-geschiedenis-van-nederland/webthemas/bevrijding-van-nederland-1944-1945/grote-lijnen/operatie-market-garden).

Every scenario starts with empty town buildings, separated town footprints and non-overlapping authored units. Ordinary starting ground units are on land connected to one of their own towns. The normal 50% opening-force reduction applies. Terrain and previews are generated entirely in code.
