# Historical scenario notes

These are historically inspired RTS missions. Terrain, distances, army sizes, chronology and unit roles are adapted to Simple Wars. Tactical outcomes are player-driven. The game has no aircraft or minefield simulation: Midway abstracts air groups with its existing unit archetypes, and El Alamein represents defensive belts with forts. Normandy landing infantry uses marine rules to model landing craft; ordinary armor begins on supplied dry land.

## Era battles

Four battles for each older era, built by `scripts/era-battles.js`: attacks, defences and, at Salamis and Trafalgar, a fleet action. Each is fought with its era's own troops: hoplites, men-at-arms or line infantry; cavalry, mounted men-at-arms or dragoons; skirmishers with javelins, rangers with bows or chasseurs; chariots, knights or heavy horse; triremes. The armies start at their authored strength (`mission.fullStartingArmy`) and keep their names. Every side has the gold to pay its upkeep and recruits with limited manpower; in the attacks the defenders raise a quarter of their towns' usual manpower (`mission.enemyManpowerRate`). Scripted waves stand for the formations of the day. The shop sells each one only to a player who owns that era's army as well as the gold.

- **Gaugamela: Alexander Charges — 1 October 331 BCE (ancient, attack).** Capture Darius's camp. Scythed chariots charge the phalanx; Bessus rides around the right; when Mazaeus attacks the left, the Persian line swings onto the Bumodus ford and a gap opens in front of the camp. [Livius on Gaugamela](https://www.livius.org/articles/battle/gaugamela-331-bce/).
- **Thermopylae: The Hot Gates — 480 BCE (ancient, defence).** Hold the Hot Gates for five minutes. The coast road narrows to four cells between the Malian Gulf and the Kallidromos cliffs; Persian ships shoot from the gulf; the Anopaia path brings a column down behind the pass. The historical battle ended in Greek defeat; a win here is a successful delaying action. [Herodotus on Thermopylae, translated extracts](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/herodotus/herodotus-on-thermopylae/).
- **Salamis: The Narrow Strait — September 480 BCE (ancient, fleet).** Sink the Persian fleet. The strait between Salamis and Attica is six or seven cells wide; the Persian squadrons crowd in from the Saronic Gulf past Psyttaleia, an Egyptian squadron comes down the western channel behind the Greeks, and the Aeginetans wait at the mouth of the strait. Persian archers watch from the Attic shore. Ship counters stand for squadrons. [Livius on Salamis](https://www.livius.org/articles/battle/salamis-480-bce/).
- **Cannae: The Double Envelopment — 2 August 216 BCE (ancient, defence).** Hold Cannae for five minutes. The legions, packed deep, drive at Hannibal's crescent of Gauls and Spaniards; the Libyans wait on the flanks, Hasdrubal's horse beats the Roman cavalry along the Aufidus, and the Numidians come back to close the trap. Holding the town stands for keeping the centre from breaking. [Battle of Cannae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cannae).
- **Hastings: Senlac Ridge — 14 October 1066 (medieval, attack).** Capture Senlac ridge and Caldbec Hill. The housecarls hold the crest behind a shield wall; the fyrd on the English right breaks ranks to chase the Normans down the slope. [English Heritage battlefield history](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/1066-battle-of-hastings-abbey-and-battlefield/history-and-stories/what-happened-battle-hastings/).
- **Agincourt: The Muddy Field — 25 October 1415 (medieval, defence).** Hold Maisoncelle for five minutes. A ploughed, rain-soaked field narrows between Agincourt and Tramecourt woods to the English line on a low rise behind its stakes; French knights and men-at-arms wade through the mud under the longbows, and riders raid the baggage. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Battle-of-Agincourt).
- **Stirling Bridge: Catch Them Crossing — 11 September 1297 (medieval, attack).** Capture Stirling and the English camp. The English must cross the Forth by one narrow bridge onto a causeway through the carse; the Scots charge down from the Abbey Craig when the vanguard is over, and Scots horse can ride round by the ford at Drip upstream. The bridge is a real bridge on the map. [Battle of Stirling Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stirling_Bridge).
- **Hattin: The Springs — 4 July 1187 (medieval, defence).** Hold Kafr Hattin and Tiberias for five minutes as Saladin, between the thirsty crusader army and the water. Count Raymond charges for the lake, the king's foot gathers on the Horns of Hattin, and the knights make their last charges. Thirst and the brush fires appear in the briefings only. [Battle of Hattin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hattin).
- **Austerlitz: The Pratzen Heights — 2 December 1805 (Napoleonic, attack).** Seize the Pratzen Heights, then take Austerlitz. The Allied southern column leaves the plateau for Telnitz, Davout arrives to hold it, the fog lifts and the Russian Guard counterattacks. [Fondation Napoléon tactical account](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/the-battle-of-austerlitz-and-the-principles-of-war/).
- **Waterloo: Hold the Ridge — 18 June 1815 (Napoleonic, defence).** Hold Mont-Saint-Jean for five minutes. Reille attacks Hougoumont, d'Erlon the centre, Ney leads the cavalry and the Imperial Guard makes the last attack before the Prussians arrive from the east. [National Army Museum account](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-waterloo).
- **Borodino: The Great Redoubt — 7 September 1812 (Napoleonic, attack).** Capture Bagration's flèches and the Great Redoubt. The Russian earthworks are forts on a low rise and on the Kurgan height, with the Semyonovka ravine behind the flèches; Bagration counterattacks, Cossacks raid across the Kolocha behind the French left, and Kutuzov sends his reserves from Gorki. [Battle of Borodino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Borodino).
- **Trafalgar: Break the Line — 21 October 1805 (Napoleonic, fleet).** Sink the Combined Fleet. Nelson's two columns attack a long French and Spanish line off Cape Trafalgar; Dumanoir's van turns back late and Gravina's squadron joins from the south. The British crews are veterans. Gibraltar is drawn much closer than it is. [Battle of Trafalgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trafalgar).

## Modern missions

The six standalone modern missions are authored the same way as the era battles, by `scripts/modern-battles.js`: small maps, whole named armies, gold for upkeep and manpower-limited recruiting, scripted waves, and a quarter of the usual manpower for the defenders in the attacks. The first four keep the IDs players bought.

- **El Alamein: Breakthrough — 23 October–4 November 1942 (attack).** Capture Tel el Eisa and Tel el Aqqaqir. Open desert between the Mediterranean and the cliffs of the Qattara Depression; Axis minefields are forts that slow the attacker and shelter the defenders; 21st Panzer comes north to close the breach and Operation Supercharge adds fresh armour. [National Army Museum account](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-alamein).

- **Normandy: Link the Beachheads — 6–12 June 1944 (attack).** Capture Carentan and Bayeux. Landings at Utah, Omaha and Gold, with assault waves in their landing craft (marine rules), the bombardment fleet offshore, bunkers on the bluffs, the Vire estuary between Utah and Omaha, and Panzer Lehr counterattacking from Saint-Lo. [National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/d-day), [Carentan local history](https://carentanlesmarais.fr/decouvrir/dday/).
- **Kursk: Operation Citadel — 5–12 July 1943 (defence).** Hold Kursk and Oboyan for five minutes against Model from Orel and Hoth from Belgorod; the II SS Panzer Corps swings onto Prokhorovka and meets the 5th Guards Tank Army. [The Tank Museum](https://tankmuseum.org/article/background-battle-kursk).
- **Midway — 4–7 June 1942 (fleet).** Sink the Japanese warships around Midway atoll: the four carriers and their battleships, then the invasion force's escort. Ship counters stand for task groups and their aircraft; the American groups are veterans for their experienced air groups. Transports that run do not have to be sunk. [US Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/world-war-ii/1942/midway.html).
- **Stalingrad: The Volga Bank — September–November 1942 (defence).** Hold Mamayev Kurgan and the central landing for five minutes with the Volga at your back. The ruined city is untagged built-up ground that shelters whoever holds it; the 13th Guards cross the river, German armour drives along the Tsaritsa gorge for the landing and fresh divisions attack past the Tractor Factory, while gunboats of the Volga flotilla support from the river. [Battle of Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad).
- **Bastogne: Break the Ring — 19–26 December 1944 (attack).** Capture Assenois and keep Bastogne. You command both the surrounded 101st Airborne and the 4th Armored Division fighting up from the south through Fallschirmjäger dug in on the roads; the besiegers attack the perimeter from Champs and Noville. [Siege of Bastogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Bastogne).

## Ordered campaign packs

Each stage keeps its individually drawn map (`scripts/historical-battlefields.js`) and fields its own armies (`scripts/historical-campaigns.js`): named formations for each side (Soviet, US, British, airborne, German) mixing infantry, armour, motorised troops and engineers or rangers, a small garrison in every defended town, enemy reserves that reinforce the front objective (two infantry companies without armour in each pack's opening stage), and two reserves for the player. Armies start at full strength, every side can pay its upkeep, and the defenders raise a quarter of their usual manpower in the attacks and half in the Arnhem hold and the Oosterbeek withdrawal. Objectives are listed nearest the assembly area first. A town's streets, every one of which must be held to take it, are its 13-block centre; the rest of a drawn built-up district keeps its urban cover and its side's ownership but no longer counts toward the capture (whole Berlin districts of up to 689 blocks used to).

Purchase a pack once, then play it under **Missions → Store**. Only stage one starts unlocked. A victory unlocks the next stage and saves progress locally and to the connected profile. Defeats and surrenders leave the current stage available. Unlocked stages can be replayed without consuming the pack. The end-of-battle button opens the next stage’s briefing and preview. Original single-mission IDs, prices and one-shot entitlements remain intact.

These packs connect tactical perspectives across an operation; they do not claim that the same formation fought every sector. Simultaneous battles can overlap in date. Mission durations compress historical time. Generic reinforcement announcements describe game events, not purported historical dispatches.

### Berlin 1945: The Final Offensive — 350 gold

Seven sectors: Seelow Heights (16–19 April), northeastern districts (21–23 April), Köpenick–Neukölln (23–26 April), southern districts (26–28 April), Moabit/Spree (28–29 April), Reichstag (30 April–1 May), central sectors and garrison surrender (1–2 May). Districts and individual buildings are scaled town objectives; the Reichstag’s capture is distinguished from the end of the battle and the later general German surrender.

References: [German Historical Museum](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/der-zweite-weltkrieg/kriegsverlauf/schlacht-um-berlin-1945), [Bundesarchiv situation map](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/dokumente-zur-zeitgeschichte/lagekarte-zur-schlacht-um-berlin/), [Berlin historical context](https://www.berlin.de/landesdenkmalamt/denkmale/highlight-denkmale-der-alliierten/die-alliierten-in-berlin/berlin-unter-verwaltung-der-vier-maechte-1945-1948-646239.php).

### Normandy: D-Day to Cherbourg — 300 gold

Five perspectives: Operation Tonga (6 June before dawn), Omaha (6 June), Gold/Juno/Sword (6–7 June), Carentan (10–13 June), Cherbourg (22–26 June). Airborne troops begin on land. Omaha assault infantry uses marine rules to represent landing craft, not Marine Corps troops. Small western beachheads supply the landing scenarios; the old artificial offshore depot islands have been removed. Caen is not a D-Day capture. Capturing Cherbourg’s harbor objective does not make the damaged historical port immediately operational.

References: [National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/d-day), [British Army on the airborne operation](https://www.army.mod.uk/news/by-air-to-battle-on-d-day/), [ABMC on the Utah sector](https://www.abmc.gov/history/discover-the-history-of-utah-beach-american-memorial/), [Carentan](https://carentanlesmarais.fr/decouvrir/dday/), [US Army, Utah Beach to Cherbourg](https://history.army.mil/Publications/Publications-Catalog/Utah-Beach-to-Cherbourg/), [Cherbourg history](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/assault-on-fortress-cherbourg-june-1944).

### Market Garden: Bridges and Withdrawal — 300 gold

Five missions: the ground corridor (17 September), Son’s broken bridge (18–19 September), the Arnhem north-end perimeter (19–21 September), Nijmegen/Waal (20 September), and the Oosterbeek withdrawal, Operation Berlin (25–26 September). Arnhem is a timed delaying action. The final mission requires ferrying at least three original 1st Airborne formations across the Lower Rhine and unloading them after the three-minute nightfall marker; newly trained troops do not count. Winning this evacuation is not presented as a successful Allied capture of Arnhem. The Waal assault-boat units represent US Army airborne troops using marine movement rules.

References: [National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/market-garden), [UK Ministry of Defence campaign booklet](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/30056/ww2_market_garden.pdf), [Netherlands Institute of Military History](https://www.nimh.nl/militaire-geschiedenis-van-nederland/webthemas/bevrijding-van-nederland-1944-1945/grote-lijnen/operatie-market-garden).

Every scenario starts with empty town buildings, separated town footprints and non-overlapping authored units. Ordinary starting ground units are on land connected to one of their own towns. The normal 50% opening-force reduction applies. Terrain and previews are generated entirely in code.

## Individually authored geography

Each of the seventeen campaign stages now has its own plan in `scripts/historical-battlefields.js`. Water courses and town coordinates are unique for every stage; changing a random seed is not used as a substitute for designing a different battlefield. Maps remain compressed, north-up tactical interpretations rather than survey maps or exact historical frontlines.

- **Seelow:** Oder meanders, the Alte Oder, low floodplain, western escarpment and southwest hinterland.
- **Northeastern Berlin:** separate street districts, Weißensee lake, the Panke and park ground.
- **Southeastern Berlin:** Spree/Dahme junction, Müggelsee and wooded banks, with approaches toward Treptow and Neukölln.
- **Southern Berlin:** the Tempelhof airfield apron, Schöneberg blocks and Landwehrkanal crossings.
- **Moabit:** the Spree bend, Humboldthafen channel and government-quarter approach, with Tiergarten to the south.
- **Reichstag:** a closer Spree crossing, open Königsplatz, government buildings and wooded Tiergarten.
- **Central Berlin:** dense central blocks between the Spree and Landwehrkanal, with separate Mitte, Potsdamer Platz and Chancellery sectors.
- **Tonga:** a straighter man-made canal beside a winding Orne, Dives on the eastern flank, airborne ground east of the bridge objectives.
- **Omaha:** concave shoreline, bluff belt, three exit valleys and inland hedgerow ground.
- **Eastern beaches:** three distinct coastal sectors, Seulles drainage and the Orne estuary.
- **Carentan:** the Douve/Merderet system, flooded meadows, northern causeway and eastern estuary.
- **Cherbourg:** headlands around a recessed roadstead, the Divette valley and inland Roule heights.
- **Ground corridor:** canal bridgehead, diagonal advance road, wooded heath and the Dommel flank.
- **Son:** long canal reaches, the broken road crossing and a curving Dommel tributary; the western detour crossing is a gameplay allowance.
- **Arnhem:** north-end urban perimeter backed by a broad Lower Rhine bend, with polder south of the river.
- **Nijmegen:** a wide Waal bend, western assault-boat positions, two bridge approaches and southeastern high ground.
- **Oosterbeek:** wooded northern perimeter and a bridge-free river evacuation to the southern Driel area.

The modern standalone scenarios keep their individual settings. Kursk gains a Seim valley and bridges; Midway has separate elongated islands and no road across the lagoon. Named geographic features and authored road bends survive scenario import/export.

Additional terrain references: [UK Ministry of Defence operation map](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/30056/ww2_market_garden.pdf), [US Army terrain account for the Douve and Merderet](https://history.army.mil/Portals/143/Images/Publications/Publication%20By%20Title%20Images/U%20Pdf/CMH_Pub_100-12.pdf), [National Museum of the Royal Navy D-Day map](https://dday.nmrn.org.uk/), [Bundesarchiv Berlin map](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/dokumente-zur-zeitgeschichte/lagekarte-zur-schlacht-um-berlin/).
